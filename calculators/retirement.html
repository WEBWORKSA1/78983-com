Retirement Savings Calculator

How much will you have, and what monthly income can it support? This calculator projects savings to your retirement age, adjusts for inflation and estimates a sustainable withdrawal using a rule-of-thumb rate.

Estimates only — not financial advice. Results depend entirely on the assumptions you enter. Last reviewed October 27, 2026.

How to use the retirement calculator

  1. Enter your current age and target retirement age.
  2. Add current savings and your monthly contribution.
  3. Set an expected return and inflation rate.
  4. Choose a withdrawal rate — 4% is a widely cited starting point, not a guarantee.

The formula

Real value = Nominal balance ÷ (1 + inflation)^years; Income ≈ Real value × withdrawal rate ÷ 12

Inflation-adjusting the result shows what the nest egg will buy in today's prices — the number that actually matters.

Worked example

At 32 with 40,000 saved, contributing 800 a month at 6.5% until 62 produces a large nominal balance — but after 2.5% inflation, its purchasing power is far smaller. That real figure × 4% ÷ 12 is your estimated monthly income in today's money.

Tips from the numbers

  • Capture any employer match first — it's an instant return.
  • Raise contributions with every pay rise; you won't miss money you never got used to.
  • Government pensions and benefits add to this income — include them in your plan.
  • Revisit annually; small course corrections beat big late ones.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 4% rule?

A guideline from historical studies suggesting a 4% initial withdrawal, adjusted for inflation, has often lasted about 30 years. It is not guaranteed and depends on markets and fees.

Why adjust for inflation?

A dollar in 30 years buys much less than today. The inflation-adjusted figure tells you real lifestyle.

Is a 6–7% return realistic?

It depends on your asset mix and future markets. Many planners use lower assumptions to build in a safety margin.

Should I include my home?

Usually not, unless you plan to sell or downsize to fund retirement.
