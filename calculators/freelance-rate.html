Freelance Hourly Rate Calculator

Most new freelancers undercharge because they forget taxes, expenses and non-billable time. Work backward from the income you want to the minimum rate you must charge.

Estimates only — not financial advice. Results depend entirely on the assumptions you enter. Last reviewed October 27, 2026.

How to use the freelance rate calculator

  1. Enter your target take-home income.
  2. Add yearly business expenses — software, equipment, insurance.
  3. Set your effective tax rate.
  4. Be honest about billable hours — admin and sales take real time.

The formula

Rate = (Income ÷ (1 − tax) + Expenses) ÷ (Billable hours/week × (52 − weeks off))

This is a floor, not a market rate. Price on value where you can.

Worked example

To take home 90,000 with 9,000 in expenses, 28% tax and 25 billable hours for 46 weeks, you need roughly 120 per hour — far above a naive 'salary ÷ 2,080 hours'.

Tips from the numbers

  • Most freelancers bill 50–70% of working hours.
  • Raise rates for new clients first.
  • Offer packages or retainers to smooth income.

Frequently asked questions

Why not divide salary by 2,080?

Employees get paid holidays, benefits and employer tax contributions; freelancers pay for all of that themselves.

Hourly or project pricing?

Hourly is simple; project or value pricing rewards efficiency. Use this rate as a floor for estimates.

How often should I raise prices?

Review at least yearly and when demand exceeds your capacity.
